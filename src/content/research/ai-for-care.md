## Technology for caregivers

Dementia changes life for the person who has it and for the family members who care for them, often around the clock. In Xiaopeng Zhao's SMART Lab at the University of Tennessee, Knoxville, I built software meant to lighten that load.

One project was a chatbot for caregivers. Rather than answering from memory alone, it first searches a library of documents and then answers from what it finds, a design called retrieval-augmented generation. I built it with LangChain, OpenAI's models and a Pinecone vector database. I also built assistive web apps that use embeddings to power custom AI features, and control systems that let the lab's social robots interact with people and guide them through tasks.

Our paper in _Frontiers in Robotics and AI_ describes how AI-powered social robots can support dementia caregivers. The robot control system I built was also used in a study of a robot-facilitated assessment of everyday self-care skills for people with Alzheimer's disease.

## REACH: genetic-risk assessment where counselors are scarce

Knowing your inherited risk of cancer can change the screening and care you receive, but genetic counselors are scarce in many rural communities. REACH (Rural Expansion of Access to Genetic Counseling and Healthcare through AI Innovations) explores whether a social robot, paired with AI, can walk people through a genetic risk assessment.

Our paper on using social robots and AI for cancer genetic-risk assessment appears in the AAAI Symposium Series. REACH was selected for a UT Grand Challenges grant, and in February 2025 I demonstrated it with Tyler Morris at the UT Grand Challenges Summit.

## From robot to app

My senior design project grew out of the same work. Dr. Zhao's social robot, FRED, became the starting point for a companion app for people living with dementia, which you can read about on my [projects page](/projects/).

## What it led to

This work taught me to build software that clinicians, patients and families depend on. The same idea, technology that fits the person using it, is at the center of my work on personalizing cochlear implants.
